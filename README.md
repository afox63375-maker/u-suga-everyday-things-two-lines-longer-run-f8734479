# A Lift Is a Bucket You Can Climb Into

A two-line explanation of an everyday thing — a lift (elevator) — written for a 10-year-old, for the make.fun bounty "Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run".

## The entry

> A lift is a little room you can stand in that rides up and down a building on a steel rope, like a bucket in a well that you climb into instead of filling with water.
>
> The bucket maps to the lift car and the rope to the steel cables that pull it, but it leaves out the counterweight, a heavy block on the other end doing most of the lifting, and the brakes that catch the car if a cable ever snaps.

## How it meets the judge brief, in the judge's own order

1. **It really explains an everyday thing to a 10-year-old.** A lift is something every child has ridden; line 1 says what it is and how it moves, in plain words.
2. **The analogy is concrete and surprising.** A bucket in a well is something a child can see, hold and picture — and meeting it as a room you climb into is a joining the reader has not met before.
3. **Line 1 is one vivid sentence.** A single sentence lands the whole picture: room, rope, building, bucket, well. No list of comparisons, no second metaphor crowding it.
4. **Line 2 is honest and specific about the mapping.** It names exactly what maps (bucket → car, rope → cables) and two real things the analogy leaves out (the counterweight and the safety brakes) — it never praises the analogy.
5. **The pair works together.** Line 2 explains line 1 instead of repeating it, in language a 10-year-old follows without being talked down to.

The analogy is also true: a lift really does hang from steel cables and travel up and down a shaft, just as a bucket hangs from a rope and travels up and down a well — so the picture leaves the child with the right idea, not a wrong one.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt`, validates them (exactly 2 lines, 1–400 characters, non-blank), reads the file back and verifies it byte-for-byte.
